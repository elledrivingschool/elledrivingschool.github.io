A mock test is the closest thing to your driving test without it counting. We drive to {{ site.test_centre }}, where the real test is taken, and I play the examiner: I say no more than an examiner would, and mark you the same way.

I follow the format the DVSA recommends in its Ready to Pass? campaign, so every part of the real test is covered. Afterwards we go through the result together, fault by fault.

What the mock test covers

  1. Licence and eyesight check. You read a number plate from 20 metres, as you will on the day.
  2. "Show me, tell me" questions. The vehicle safety questions an examiner asks.
  3. General driving. The kinds of road the test uses around {{ site.test_centre }}: country roads, town roads, dual carriageways, multi-lane roundabouts and one-way systems.
  4. Reversing. One of the manoeuvres from the real test.
  5. Independent driving. Around 20 minutes following a sat nav or road signs.
  6. An emergency stop.
  7. Your result and feedback. Marked as driving faults, serious faults and dangerous faults, the way the real test is.

Am I ready for my driving test?

The DVSA's Ready to Pass? campaign gives five signs that a learner is ready. In my words:

  • You drive without needing prompts from your instructor.
  • You are not making serious or dangerous mistakes.
  • You can pass mock tests.
  • You have practised ways of keeping your nerves under control.
  • Your instructor agrees that you are ready.

A mock test answers the third one directly and tells you a good deal about the others. If nerves are the part that worries you, read about lessons for nervous drivers.

Who mock tests are for

  • My own pupils, before I say you are ready to book.
  • Learners with another instructor who want a second opinion.
  • Learners practising with a parent or partner who want to know where they stand.
  • Anyone with a test date coming up who wants to know what to work on first.
